## 1. What the tuple means

`_linearity_error(tilt)` is the relative L2 violation of linearity of the whole
chain on a 2x2 order fan of half-angle `tilt`: `||chain(sum_k E_k) - sum_k
chain(E_k)|| / ||sum_k chain(E_k)||`.  Physical propagation is linear, so this
is an oracle-free measure of how wrong the multiplexed route is -- the route
the D3 gate exists to refuse.

The failing body measured three of them and asserted two ratios:

| symbol | what it is |
|---|---|
| `bad6` | fan at +-23 mrad, shipped `_REMAP_RESID_EIKONAL_DEGREE = 6` |
| `good6` | pair at +-0.5 mrad, same degree |
| `bad4` | fan at +-23 mrad, degree forced to 4 |

* claim 1, `bad6 > 5 * good6` -- the gate still separates;
* claim 2, `bad4 > 5 * bad6` -- **the residual degree is what moves the
  multiplexed route**.  This is the one that failed: CI got 21.0394 against
  21.4420, i.e. the two degrees produced the SAME wrongness (0.98x).

The whole body ran with `_lens_traced.LSTSQ_CONDITIONING_STEPDOWN = False`
(the pre-C13 solver), an era pin added by niche C13 on 2026-08-03.

## 3. Hypotheses put up and knocked down

### 3.1 RAM-bounded internal grid -- ELIMINATED

The library sizes fine grids against `get_ram_budget()`
(`_memory_bounded_n_fine`), and a 12.9 GB runner could plausibly pick a
coarser grid than any dev box.  It does not happen here.

* **Spy on `_memory_bounded_n_fine` over a full `_linearity_error(0.023)`:
  `n_calls = 0`.**  `_chain` pins `final_leg='paraxial'` and
  `_linearity_error` passes `focus_readout=None`, so neither the exact focus
  readout nor `_fine_trace_group_exit` is entered and there is no fine grid to
  size.
* With `lumenairy.set_max_ram(12.9 GiB)` installed before import, `bad6`
  (step-down off) reads `1.1206206091954127` -- **bit-identical** to the
  unlimited run.
* At that budget the cap first binds above `n_req = 8192`
  (`256..8192 -> unchanged`, `16384 -> 8192`).  The largest grid in this
  fixture is `N = 512`.

### 3.2 The two "0.8757" energy-band messages -- NOT THIS TEST, AND NOT A CLIFF

The same CI run carries two copies of

```text
apply_real_lens_traced: amplitude_model='ray_density' energy self-check FAILED
-- exit power / aperture-transmitted input power = 0.875777 / 0.875764,
outside the documented band [0.8900, 1.0500] for ray_subsample=4.
```

They are **not** from this test's chain calls.  In the log they sit in the
py3.10 shard-1 warnings summary attributed to
`tests/unit/test_niche_p2_guards.py::test_self_check_dx_flags_a_non_convergent_chain`,
**which passed**.  That fixture is a deliberately non-convergent chain -- its
own docstring: *"a chain whose INPUT CARRIER is beyond the grid Nyquist (a
steep diverging conjugate on a coarse grid) is genuinely not dx-stable --
refining moves the readout power by ~50%"* -- and the two messages are its two
`self_check='dx'` legs, `N=768 / dx=4.000 um` and `N=1086 / dx=2.829 um`,
which are the two grids named in the surrounding warnings.

Run here, that test emits **0.875777 and 0.875764** on BOTH dev builds -- the
same six digits as CI, in the same order, on three different machines:

| | first leg (N=768) | second leg (N=1086) | test |
|---|---|---|---|
| GitHub CI, ubuntu-latest py3.10 | 0.875777 | 0.875764 | PASSED |
| Windows / scipy-openblas Haswell | 0.875777 | 0.875764 | PASSED |
| WSL / OpenBLAS | 0.875777 | 0.875764 | PASSED |

So:

* the messages are build-INDEPENDENT, i.e. there is no "CI ran a leg in a
  different regime";
* they are not RAM-driven either -- the fine-grid cap does not bind at N=768
  or N=1086 on a 12.9 GB budget (S3.1);
* they are the intended symptom of a fixture built to be non-convergent, and
  the test that owns them asserts the guard fires.

**The silent-accuracy-cliff finding is therefore NOT real on this evidence**,
and `lumenairy/propagators/carrier.py` is not modified.  (The general question
-- whether a RAM-bounded grid can silently fall below the validated envelope --
is not settled by this; what is settled is that it did not happen in this CI
run and is not what these messages are.)

## 4. The mechanism

### 4.1 The census

Instrumenting `_solve_lstsq_thread_safe` over one full `_linearity_error`
evaluation of this fixture:

| | solves | with equilibrated Gram rcond `== 0.0` | worst `\|\|b - A x_returned\|\| / \|\|b - A x_QR\|\|` |
|---|---|---|---|
| step-down OFF (the era pin), Windows | 35 | **30** | **1.10e6** |
| step-down OFF, WSL | 35 | **30** | **4.22e6** |
| step-down ON (shipped), Windows | 35 | 30 | 1.0000008 |
| step-down ON, WSL | 35 | 30 | 1.0000001 |

`_gram_rcond` returns exactly `0.0` when the equilibrated Gram has lost
positive-definiteness outright, so 30 of the 35 fits behind every arm of the
era-pinned body were answered by a Cholesky solve of a matrix that is not
positive definite in float64.  `LSTSQ_CONDITIONING_STEPDOWN`'s own note in
`_lens_traced.py` states the consequence: *"the Cholesky answer is an
arbitrary draw from the numerical null space rather than the least-squares
solution"*, and *"the draw's fit residual runs 15-23x above the attainable
minimum on one build and 1.05x on the other, from the SAME source at the SAME
degree."*  On this fixture the excess is six orders, not one.

`tests/unit/test_niche_c13_lstsq_conditioning.py` already asserts the same
property at the matrix level: `TestTheContract::
test_the_normal_equations_miss_the_minimum_on_the_weighted_fit` bars it at 10x
on a synthetic two-scale fit.  What is new here is the count -- on the D3 chain
it is not one fit but 30 of 35, and the excess is 1e6 rather than 1e1.

### 4.2 What the bar was actually barring

The ratio `bad4 / bad6`, era-pinned:

| build | `bad4` | `bad6` | `bad4 / bad6` |
|---|---|---|---|
| **CI, py3.11 / numpy 2.4.6** | **21.0394** | **21.4420** | **0.98x  <- FAILED** |
| CI, py3.10 / numpy 2.2.6 | -- | -- | passed |
| Windows, 24 threads | 15.6984 | 1.1206 | 14.01x |
| WSL, 1 thread | 21.9670 | 0.6631 | 33.13x |
| WSL, 2 threads | 21.4269 | 0.6631 | 32.31x |

A 34x disagreement about a quantity barred at 5x.  Note also that CI's `bad4`
(21.0394) is within 0.4 % of WSL's SHIPPED-era `bad4` (21.0315): the numerator
is a well-behaved quantity on every build.  All of the failure is in `bad6`,
which reads 0.66 / 1.12 / **21.44** depending on who ran it.

### 4.3 It is a draw, not chaos

Perturbing every chain input by a relative `1e-13` -- far below any physical
tolerance, at the level two BLAS reduction orders differ by -- and re-running
`bad6` twice on Windows:

| | clean | seed 1 | seed 2 | spread |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| step-down OFF | 1.1206 | 1.0517 | 1.1494 | 1.09x |
| step-down ON | 1.2606 | 1.0625 | 1.0594 | 1.19x |

So the value is NOT hypersensitive to input noise within one build; it is
selected by the arithmetic implementation, is stable per build, and jumps when
the implementation changes.  That is exactly why the test passed reproducibly
on Windows and in WSL sweeps and failed reproducibly on one CI job -- "it
passes on my box" carried no information at all about it.

### 4.4 And the draw is chosen by the ROUTINE -- reproduced locally

The point above, demonstrated without leaving one machine.  With the step-down
off the singular Gram is answered by SciPy's `cho_factor` / `cho_solve`.  Swap
that for NumPy's LU (`np.linalg.solve`) -- **the same normal equations**, and
the other routine `_solve_lstsq_thread_safe` already falls back to when
Cholesky refuses -- and re-run both arms in the SAME process, same tree, same
BLAS, same threads:

| one Windows process, one tree | `bad6` | `bad4` | `bad4 / bad6` |
|---|---|---|---|
| era-pinned, Cholesky (the shipped pre-C13 path) | 1.1206 | 15.6984 | 14.01x |
| era-pinned, LU (`np.linalg.solve`) | **0.4489** | 15.3162 | **34.12x** |
| change | **2.50x** | 2.4 % | **2.44x** |
| shipped step-down (control, same run) | 1.2606 | 19.0846 | 15.14x |

One LAPACK routine choice moves `bad6` by 2.5x and the barred ratio by 2.4x,
with `bad4` essentially fixed.  Neither answer is more correct than the other:
both are the normal equations on a Gram that is not positive definite.  A CI
image that ships a different NumPy (2.2.6 vs 2.4.6) changes exactly this, and
there is no reason the resulting draw should land above 5x.  The control row
returns the standing shipped-era numbers to the last bit, so the swap is the
only thing that moved.

### 4.5 This was foreseen and half-acted-on

`docs/audits/C13_DEGREE6_CONDITIONING_2026_08_03.md` S11.8 records the same
test going red on Linux at C13 time, tabulates the shipped-era ratio as
**6.78x (Windows) / 3.20x (Linux)** against the 5x bar, and concludes with the
right principle: *"where C13 removes a build-dependence, the right response is
to assert the new invariant, not to loosen the old bar."*  It applied that to
claim 1 (the new `test_c13_makes_the_d3_separation_build_independent`, barred
at 100x) and, for claim 2, era-pinned the old bar instead -- putting the
assertion back inside the lottery.  This fix finishes that job.

This is the THIRD cross-build casualty of this one test.
`C11_PHYSICAL_DECENTRE_GATE_2026_08_03.md` S9.x records the first ("its own
first revision was ALSO a cross-build casualty, which is worth recording
because the fix is the interesting part") and moved claim 2 from the `good`
arm to the `bad` arm; C13 S11.8 records the second and era-pinned it.  Both
moved WHERE the claim was read.  Neither asked whether the arithmetic under it
could answer at all, which is what S4.1 measures.

## 5. The fix

`tests/unit/test_niche_d3_guards.py` only.

**(a) The era pin is removed.**  Both claims are now measured on the SHIPPED
solver, where the census reads 1.0000008:

| shipped solver | `bad4` | `bad6` | `bad4 / bad6` |
|---|---|---|---|
| Windows, 24 threads | 19.0846 | 1.2606 | 15.14x |
| WSL, 1 thread | 21.0315 | 0.7305 | 28.79x |
| WSL, 2 threads | 21.5216 | 0.7305 | 29.46x |

**(b) Claim 2's bar moves 5.0x -> 2.0x**, and claim 1's stays at 5.0x.  2.0x
clears every value ever measured for this ratio in the shipped solver -- the
15x-29x above and the 6.78x / 3.20x recorded on the 2026-08-03 tree -- with
1.6x of headroom on the weakest of them.  This is not a weakened bar: what it
replaces is a bar on a quantity that is not a property of the library.  Claim
1 at 5.0x has 12.5x of headroom on the weakest shipped-era value measured here
(62.5x, WSL) and 3.4x on the weakest ever recorded for it (17.2x, pre-C13
Linux).

**(c) A new sibling carries the attribution EXACTLY**, so claim 2 no longer
rests on a magnitude at all:

```python
def test_the_residual_degree_moves_the_multiplexed_route_only_through_c6():
```

`_REMAP_RESID_EIKONAL_DEGREE` is read in exactly one place -- the
`_fit_residual_eikonal` call in `apply_real_lens_traced`'s niche-C6 block,
guarded by `if _pip_remap and REMAP_STATIONARY_PHASE_LAUNCH and ...`.  So:

| | measured Windows | measured WSL |
|---|---|---|
| C6 launch OFF, `max\|E(deg 6) - E(deg 4)\|` | **0.0 exactly** | **0.0 exactly** |
| C6 launch ON, `\|\|E6 - E4\|\| / \|\|E6\|\|` | 39.83 | 43.88 |

The first row is **the fail-before**: with C6 off the degree is inert, so
`bad4 / bad6` reads exactly 1.0 and claim 2's assertion fails -- whatever the
arithmetic does.  Byte-identity is not a tolerance.  The second row is barred
at 1.0, forty times inside the weaker measurement.  Cost: four chain calls
(~16 s Windows, ~58 s WSL), no reference legs.

**(d) The test name is unchanged** so the `.test_durations` key survives
(that file is not writable here, and a stale split has timed out release-tag
shards before).  Runtime moves 63.7 s -> ~75 s: the same three
`_linearity_error` evaluations, ~17 % dearer each because 30 of the 35 solves
now take the C13 QR re-solve, which is the point.  The new sibling adds one
key at ~16 s.

The C13 step-down is not implicated: the census on this tree reads a worst

Timeline: the failing CI job ran at 23:07 UTC on this same test file (the
traceback's line numbers, 782 and 799, are this file's pre-fix line numbers),
and `test_c13_...` -- which `pytest-split` puts in shard 2/3 -- PASSED in all

docstring pointing at this section.  The owning change should either restore
`bad6`'s build-independence or re-derive the bar with the reason.

Note also that `bad6` at degree 6 is intrinsically the most fragile number in
this file: it is a near-cancellation (the residual model is good, so the
multiplexed route's linearity violation is small) evaluated on a field whose
ray launch is genuinely undefined at the fringe nulls.  `bad4`, `good6` and
`good4` are all stable to 0.4-13 % across builds; only `bad6` swings.  Any
future bar in this family has to survive that swing.  S5(b) does it by sitting
1.6x below the smallest ratio `bad6` has ever produced in the shipped solver
(3.20x, Linux, 2026-08-03 tree) and 7.6x below the smallest measured on this
tree (15.14x, Windows), and S5(c) does it by
taking `bad6` out of the attribution claim altogether -- the byte-identity
witness does not read a linearity error at all.
