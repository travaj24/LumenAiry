## 1. D2 -- the standoff optimum is set by the GRID EXTENT, not by NA

### 1.1 Root cause

`_FOCUS_STANDOFF_ZR = 0.8` was a CONSTANT multiple of the Rayleigh range,
justified by an argument that the optimum is NA-independent.  The argument is
sound as far as it goes -- the hand-off fringe count really does cancel NA --
but it is not the controlling term.  The controlling term is the CONTAINMENT
MARGIN at the hand-off plane, and the module's own note named it and then did
not use it (`_FOCUS_STANDOFF_BRIDGE_SAFETY` was defined and never read).

Write `ext = (N dx / 2) / w_env` for the input grid half-width in beam radii
and `f = standoff / zR`.  The carrier leg stops `f zR` short of the focus,
where the co-moving grid has contracted to `half * f zR / |z_focus|` while the
beam has grown to `w0 sqrt(1 + f^2)`.  Their ratio is

```
margin(f) = ext * f / sqrt(1 + f^2)                                     [1]
```

-- purely geometric, independent of NA and of wavelength, rising with the leg
and saturating at `ext`.

**Measured decomposition** (4 NA x 7 grid extents x 10 leg lengths, relative
L2 of `|F|` against an exact discrete paraxial focal-plane oracle -- a plain
numpy chirp-z quadrature of the same sampled pupil, no lumenairy):

```
err(f) = C(margin) + L(f)
```

* `C` is GAUSSIAN CLIPPING of the beam's halo by the contracted co-moving
  grid.  Measured **`C ~ 0.34 exp(-margin^2)`**, the SAME curve at NA 0.03 and
  NA 0.278:

  | margin | 1.72 | 2.23 | 2.68 | 3.09 | 3.75 |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | measured C | 1.6e-2 | 2.8e-3 | 2.6e-4 | 2.4e-5 | ~1e-5 (floor) |
  | `0.34 exp(-m^2)` | 1.8e-2 | 2.3e-3 | 2.6e-4 | 2.5e-5 | -- |

* `L` is the hand-off model error of the paraxial reconstruction, monotone
  INCREASING in `f` at every extent (the columns at ext 3, 4, 6 and 10 are
  identical above the clipping floor: 1.24e-2 / 1.19e-2 at f=1.4,
  1.110e-1 / 1.110e-1 at f=6) and growing with NA.

So the accurate leg is the SHORTEST one that clears the containment margin --
which is exactly the closed form the module already documented:

```
f = M / sqrt(ext^2 - M^2)      <=>     s = M w0 / sqrt(A^2 - (M w0/zR)^2)
```

with `A = half / |z_focus|`; for `s << zR` this reduces to the previously
documented `s_min = 2 _BRIDGE_FIT_MARGIN w0 |z_focus| / (N dx)`.

### 1.2 The shipped law

```
_FOCUS_STANDOFF_MARGIN       = 2.0 * _BRIDGE_FIT_MARGIN   (= 3.2)
_FOCUS_STANDOFF_WAIST_GROWTH = 2.0    -> f_cap = sqrt(3), sat = 0.866

ext    = (min(Ny,Nx) dx / 2) / w_env
M_req  = min(_FOCUS_STANDOFF_MARGIN, sat * ext)
f      = M_req / sqrt(ext^2 - M_req^2)          (<= f_cap by construction)
standoff = f zR + max(0, |z| - z_focus)
```

`M = 3.2` is not a new constant: it is the factor already written into the
module's own closed form, and it is 2.0x the near-focus bridge's own trust
threshold, so the resolved leg is structurally clear of
`_near_focus_needs_bridge` (which pins the error at a plateau).  That
clearance is what the deleted `_FOCUS_STANDOFF_BRIDGE_SAFETY = 1.25` tried to
express as a separate never-read number; **the constant is REMOVED** rather
than wired, because measurement puts it in the wrong direction.  Wiring it
would mean `M = 1.25 * 3.2 = 4.0`, and on one sweep (5 extents, cap 0.95) that
scores 6.72 / 6.82 / 6.88e-3 at the 4 / 6 / 8 `w0` windows against `M = 3.2`'s
4.23 / 4.31 / 5.04e-3 -- 1.4-1.6x worse everywhere.

The cap exists because [1] saturates: on a grid narrower than `M` beam radii
no leg can reach the margin.  `growth = 2.0` says "do not stretch the leg past
the plane where the beam has doubled", beyond which `half` and `w(s)` both
grow linearly and the margin buys nothing.

**Both parameters sit on a broad plateau, not at a calibrated point.**  Sweep
of geomean relL2 over 4 NA x 7 extents, scored on FIXED readout windows of
4 / 6 / 8 `w0` (so a short leg pays for its own replicas):

## 2. D3 -- a real replica guard on the public readouts

### 2.1 Root cause

Both public focus readouts finish with `angular_spectrum_propagate_mft`, whose
reconstruction is PERIODIC with period `N_in d_in` of whatever grid it is
handed.  For `carrier_referenced_focus_readout` that grid is the CO-MOVING one
at the stop plane, whose pitch scales with the standoff -- so the period is a
function of the leg length, and shortening the leg shortens it proportionally.
Neither public readout had any guard: they only optionally filled the private
`_period_out`.  The chain's guard is a MULTIPLEXING guard and downgrades to a
warning at `K == 1`; the only thing that fired was a downstream `UserWarning`
from `angular_spectrum_propagate_mft`, which any upstream
`filterwarnings('ignore')` removes -- the exact failure mode the module's own
`on_replica` note says `on_replica='error'` was introduced to avoid.

### 2.2 The fix

`_check_readout_replica(fn, period, dx_out, N_out, on_replica, remedy)` plus
`on_replica: str = 'error'` on BOTH `carrier_referenced_focus_readout` and
`carrier_referenced_exact_focus_readout`, validated AT ENTRY (not only at the
guard site) so a typo cannot ride through the whole leg.  `'error'` rather
than a warning because the failure is a plausible-looking wrong answer: the
spot CORE is untouched by replicas, so a width, a peak or a Strehl still reads
correctly while every wing-weighted quantity is garbage.  Not auto-adjust,
because silently returning a different window than the caller asked for is the
same disease one level down.

The message is actionable: it quotes the period, the ratio, the largest safe
`N_out`, and -- because the period is LINEAR in the standoff -- the standoff
that would cover the window asked for.  On the exact readout the knob that
moves the period is `window_factor` (the period IS the fine crop window), and
the message says so instead.

`on_replica` is forwarded from `propagate_traced_carrier_chain_multi` into the
per-congruence readout (`_window`), and suppressed in the `readout_tile='auto'`
PERIOD-PROBE pass only (refusing the probe would make the period unmeasurable
and `'auto'` unable to size a window that fits).  `'on_replica'` was added to
`_OUTPUT_GRID_PASSTHROUGH` so the single chain and per-congruence overrides can
reach it.

### 2.4 The `_TILE` reversal

The review caught `_TILE` being cut 256 -> 120 in
`test_niche_d2_chain_multi.py` -- a 2.1x reduction in the area over which
"tiled equals hand-placed" is checked, bought by the default flip.
**REVERSED to 256.**  The tile is now PAID FOR with the same physically
justified remedy that file already applied three times: a wide readout window
REQUIRES a long Bluestein leg, so `_leg_for_window(window, fd)` inverts
`period = N dx standoff / |z_focus|` and asks for the leg that holds the tile
(30% margin).  The tile size is now independent of whatever the accuracy
resolver picks, which is the point.

The three pre-existing fixture opt-outs (`standoff = 1.3 * window * fd /
(N dx)` on the conjugate-fringe, fringe-period and centroid fixtures) are
**KEPT and judged CORRECT**, not bought-green: those tests need a wide window
(a +-150 um fringe analysis, a 1.024 mm tile, a wing-sensitive centroid), a
wide window physically requires a long leg, and the file already records that
downgrading the replica guard instead was measured to corrupt the recombined
power by 2.4%.  Coupling the leg to the window is exactly what a CALLER who
needs a wide window should do knowingly -- it is only wrong as a library
DEFAULT, because it would make the propagated field depend on how wide a
window it is viewed through.

### 2.5 The waived call sites, and why each is legitimate

`on_replica='ignore'` was added at eight fixtures (the seven below plus
`c1_consolidation`'s whitelist-coverage sample, whose subject is the KEY LIST
and not the guard).  In every case the window
already exceeded one period before this fix -- the guard did not create the
condition, it revealed it -- and in every case the assertions read only the
core or the bookkeeping:

| file | window / period | what is read |
|---|---|---|
| `r9_highna_final_leg` | 178 um / 12.3 um | EE inside 2 `w0` = 2.8 um, and the peak (the PARAXIAL fail-before arm only) |
| `d5_dx_flatness_gate` | ladder | core FWHM / EE ladder |
| `p2_design_battery` | scan grid | FWHM + EE inside a few waists, re-propagated by the scan's own MFT |
| `d3_guards` | 358 um / 76.9 um | which GUARD fires; no field values |
| `d4_dgrating` | 1.02 mm / tile | chief-ray placement + power bookkeeping |
| `d1_tilted_carrier` | common grid | centroid / peak position on the core + power |

The DEFAULT refusal is separately pinned by four new tests in

corruption it prevents, disposition validation, and the exact readout's own

Two d2 tests whose SUBJECT is the multiplexing guard were rewritten rather
than waived.  `test_k1_keeps_the_requested_field_of_view` documented "K = 1
returns the whole requested field of view, with a warning" -- the exact
permissiveness the review called out.  It now pins BOTH halves: the default

field-of-view contract holds verbatim against the hand-placed reference.
That is more coverage, not less.

## 3. D5 -- the fit-domain gates and the basis

### 3.1 What changed under this fix, and what did not

The `newton_fit='auto'` -> spline flip that crossed the three
`newton_fit != 'spline'` gates has been **reverted by the original campaign's
author** (uncommitted, in the working tree, `_lens_traced.py` ~L5558-5578;
see `HANDOFF_TRACED_EXACT_2026_08_05_ADDENDUM.md`).  That revert is PRESERVED
here and is not this fix's work.  It closes the DEFAULT path.  It does not
close the disease: an explicit `newton_fit='spline'` still crosses all three
gates, silently.

### 3.2 Adjudication, per gate

**The disc restriction (`_lens_traced.py:7297` block) is POLYNOMIAL-SPECIFIC
AS IMPLEMENTED, and that is a fact about the code, not a preference.**  The
restriction has exactly two implementations and neither has a home on
`RectBivariateSpline`:

* D1's regularised form is least-squares WEIGHTS (`_fit_weights`), consumed
  only by `_Cheb2DEvaluator(..., weights=...)`.
  `RectBivariateSpline.__init__(x, y, z, bbox, kx, ky, s, maxit)` takes no
  weights -- checked by `inspect.signature` in the witness test.
* the historical form is a hard NaN mask outside the disc.  **Measured: one
  NaN corner sample on a 21x21 lattice makes `RectBivariateSpline.ev()` return
  NaN AT THE GRID CENTRE.**  Masking would not restrict the fit, it would
  destroy it.

And the MECHANISM the restriction exists to control -- a global total-degree
least-squares fit whose every coefficient sees every sample, extrapolating
outside its own data support -- is a property of that basis.  The library's own
pointwise scoring against an exact skew ray trace (`_REMAP_RESID_FREEZE_MARGIN`)
measures it: polynomial 5.608 / 15.079 um in the skirt / at the aperture rim,
spline 0.006 / 0.002 um.

**The C11 arbiter and C12 predictor choose between two DISCS**, so with no disc
there is nothing to arbitrate.  They are gated on the same flag for that
structural reason, not as a separate judgement.

**What is NOT claimed is that spline is safe past the aperture:beam cliff.**
E4 corrected relay, beam `w = 2 mm` held FIXED and only the aperture grown,
N = 1536, exit-wavefront Strehl (rms in rad):

```
 ap_mm ap/beam_d |  poly frbf=None    poly frbf=2.0   spline frbf=None  spline frbf=2.0
   4.0      1.00 |  0.9983(0.041)     0.9983(0.041)   0.9983(0.041)     0.9983(0.041)
   6.0      1.50 |  0.9940(0.078)     0.9997(0.016)   ALL-ZERO FIELD    ALL-ZERO FIELD
   8.0      2.00 |  0.0420(1.781)     0.9986(0.037)   ALL-ZERO FIELD    ALL-ZERO FIELD
  10.0      2.50 |  0.0378(1.810)     0.9986(0.038)   ALL-ZERO FIELD    ALL-ZERO FIELD
```

So past the cliff the polynomial basis degrades gracefully and
`fit_radius_beam_factor=2.0` recovers it to 0.999, while the spline basis
returns an **all-zero exit field** that the knob cannot rescue -- because the
gate excludes it.  This is the addendum's "non-finite exit field", reproduced
independently and localised to the aperture axis.  It is the reason the
inertness must be ANNOUNCED rather than assumed benign.

### 3.3 The fix

1. `_fit_domain_basis_ok = (newton_fit != 'spline')` is named ONCE and both
   gates read it, so a future basis cannot pick up half of them.
2. New `on_fit_domain_basis: str = 'warn'` on `apply_real_lens_traced`.  When
   the resolved basis cannot honour a fit-domain restriction the caller asked
   for (`fit_radius_beam_factor`, `decentred_fit_poly_order`, or the
   arbiter/predictor on a decentred beam), the call SAYS SO, names which knob
   is inert, and quotes the E4 measurement.  `'error'` makes it fatal,
   `'silent'` acknowledges.  `'warn'` is the default because
   `newton_fit='spline'` is deliberately used as a FIT-DOMAIN-FREE ORACLE in
   `test_niche_d7`, `c8`, `c6` and the C11/C12 validation scripts; erroring
   would retire that idiom.
3. **The live half.**  The aperture:beam cliff warning was skipped whenever
   `_beam_fit_radius is not None`, because the disc IS the remedy -- so on the
   spline basis an INERT `fit_radius_beam_factor` SILENCED the warning about
   exactly the failure it had stopped preventing.  The suppression is now
   conditional on the restriction actually being applicable.  No behaviour
   change on the polynomial path (pinned).

### 3.4 The gate-coverage witness

`tests/unit/test_fix_d5_fit_domain_basis.py` (9 tests, ~5 s, no proprietary
asset).  One invariant in two halves, per feature:

* **A** -- on the SHIPPED default basis the feature must be LIVE: toggling
  `fit_radius_beam_factor`, or `DECENTRED_FIT_ARBITER` on niche C11's own f/3
  fixture at 0.2 w, must CHANGE the returned field.  A basis flip that makes
  it inert fails here.
* **B** -- on a basis that cannot honour it, the call must ANNOUNCE it (and
  the feature must genuinely be inert, so the announcement is not decoration).
* and the closing half: **the default basis must NEVER emit that
  announcement.**  A + B + that third assertion are mutually exclusive on the
  default path, so a future basis flip has to pick one -- and neither is
  silent.

Every bar is `array_equal` or the presence of a warning.  No tolerance, no
magnitude, no BLAS-dependent quantity.  The `RectBivariateSpline` NaN fact and
the missing `weights` parameter are pinned too, so the adjudication itself has
a tripwire: if a future SciPy makes either false, the test says the D5
decision must be redone rather than silently standing on a stale premise.

## 5. What is NOT closed

1. **The D2 law loses at ext ~2 / low NA and at ext 3-4 on FWHM** (S1.5).  The
   losing cells are named with numbers.  The ext-2 low-NA loss is a different
   code path (`_near_focus_needs_bridge`) being accidentally good there; a
   resolver that could tell when the bridge is the better route would take
   those cells too, and nothing here does that.
2. **`L(f)`, the long-leg hand-off term, is measured but not derived.**  Its
   NA and `f` scaling are empirical (`~NA^2.5 f^1.1..1.7`).  Only its
   MONOTONICITY in `f` is used by the fix, and that is measured at every
   extent -- but a derivation would let the optimum be solved rather than
   bounded.
3. **The exact readout's replica guard fires and is now pinned, but only on a
   synthetic sphere** (`test_the_exact_readout_guards_the_same_way_on_its_own_period`
   -- a 30 um beam at `window_factor=4.0` gives a 120 um period and a 204.8 um
   window is refused, naming `window_factor` rather than `standoff`).  No
   SHIPPED CHAIN configuration was found that trips it: the exact leg's period
   is `window_factor` beam radii, which on real designs is far wider than any
   window asked for.  So the guard is validated, not exercised in anger.
4. **`on_fit_domain_basis` defaults to `'warn'`, not `'error'`**, purely to
   preserve the spline-as-fit-domain-free-oracle idiom in five existing test
   files.  Given the measured all-zero exit field past the cliff, a future
   pass should consider making the combination fatal once those oracles pass
   `'silent'` explicitly.
5. **Nothing here was run on GPU, on CuPy/JAX, or through
   `apply_real_lens_traced_multi`'s prepared-screen reuse.**  `use_gpu=True`
   forces `newton_fit='polynomial'`, so the D5 flag is inert there by
   construction, but that is an argument rather than a measurement.
6. **Design 121 itself was not re-run.**  The D2 change moves the readout leg
   on every chain that takes the paraxial final leg; 121's production route
   takes the EXACT final leg, whose period is `window_factor` beam radii and
   is untouched, but the chain-A pre-DOE legs are not re-measured here.

7. **The three pre-existing wide-window fixture opt-outs in
   `test_niche_d2_chain_multi.py` are KEPT** (S2.4) on the argument that a
   wide window physically requires a long leg.  That argument is sound but the
   1.3x margin they use is not derived, and their closed form
   `standoff = 1.3 * window * fd / (N dx)` is the input-grid one that
   `_leg_for_window` had to abandon as 1.73x optimistic on this relay.  They
   pass because 1.3 happens to cover their own cases; that is luck, not
   design, and it is left as inherited debt rather than silently re-derived.
8. **The d6 EE2-ratio and paraxial-peak-offset discriminators are now thin**
   (1.11x and 1.39x headroom, S4.1).  Neither moved because of this fix, but
   the next improvement to the paraxial leg's spot placement will retire one
   of them, and the review's own note about the eroding FWHM axis applies to
   these two as well.
